# RunBook - AI Accountability Manager

## One‑Line Pitch

An AI manager that runs scheduled voice meetings to hold users accountable for their goals—just like a real workplace stand‑up.

---

## Problem Statement

Most productivity and todo apps fail not because users don’t know *what* to do, but because no one asks them *why it wasn’t done*.

Traditional todo apps:

* Rely entirely on self‑discipline
* Are easy to ignore or postpone
* Do not remember excuses or patterns
* Lack social pressure and accountability

In real workplaces, accountability comes from:

* Daily or weekly stand‑ups
* A manager who remembers commitments
* Follow‑up questions and pressure
* Pattern recognition over time

This human dynamic is missing in personal productivity tools.

---

## Core Idea

Simulate a real manager‑led meeting using AI.

Instead of checking off tasks in a list, users **attend a scheduled voice meeting** with an AI manager who:

* Knows their goals
* Remembers past meetings
* Questions incomplete work
* Identifies behavioral patterns
* Locks in new commitments

The AI does not act like a chatbot.
It acts like a professional manager running a stand‑up.

---

## What the Product Is (and Is Not)

### It IS:

* An accountability system
* A daily stand‑up simulator
* A voice‑first AI experience
* A behavioral productivity tool

### It is NOT:

* A todo app with AI chat
* A generic AI coach
* A dashboard‑heavy productivity tracker

---

## User Experience Overview

### 1. Setup

The user defines:

* Goals (daily / weekly / monthly)
* Meeting frequency (daily stand‑up, weekly review)
* Preferred meeting time
* Manager style (supportive, strict, adaptive)

Optional inputs:

* Known weaknesses (procrastination, over‑commitment)
* Areas of focus (work, learning, fitness, side projects)

---

### 2. Scheduled AI Meeting (Core Loop)

At the chosen time, the user joins a **voice call** with the AI manager.

The meeting follows a structured format:

1. **Opening**

   * Review of previous commitments

2. **Progress Check**

   * What was completed?
   * What was not?

3. **Interrogation**

   * Why was the task incomplete?
   * What specifically blocked progress?

4. **Pattern Recognition**

   * Missed deadlines
   * Repeated excuses
   * Over‑estimation trends

5. **Commitment Lock‑In**

   * Clear, time‑bound goals for the next meeting

The AI controls the flow of the meeting.

---

### 3. Post‑Meeting Output

After each meeting, the system generates:

* A short meeting summary
* Updated commitments
* Risk flags (e.g., repeated slippage)
* Adjusted expectations for next check‑in

No complex dashboards are required for the MVP.

---

## Why Voice (Not Chat)

Chat‑based accountability is easy to avoid and easy to fake.

Voice meetings:

* Increase psychological pressure
* Require real‑time thinking
* Reduce vague or dishonest answers
* Feel closer to real workplace interactions

The experience mirrors a phone or stand‑up meeting—not a chatbot conversation.

---

## AI Manager Behavior

The AI manager is defined by **rules, not vibes**.

It is instructed to:

* Reject vague answers
* Ask follow‑up questions
* Remember missed commitments
* Escalate strictness when patterns repeat
* End every meeting with a clear commitment

Tone adapts over time based on user behavior.

---

## Memory & Context

The AI maintains long‑term memory such as:

* Missed deadlines
* Common excuses
* Accuracy of time estimates
* Emotional tone and confidence levels

This memory is injected into every meeting, allowing the AI to behave consistently and authoritatively.
